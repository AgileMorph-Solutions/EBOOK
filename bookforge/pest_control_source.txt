SENIOR PEST CONTROL AT HOME — SOURCE NOTES
Practical, low-cost methods before calling a professional exterminator.

POSITIONING
This guide is for homeowners—especially seniors on fixed incomes—who want to try safe, inexpensive remedies before paying $200–$800+ for a single exterminator visit. Many household pests can be reduced or prevented with diligence, sealing, sanitation, and common pantry items. Professional help remains necessary for severe infestations, bed bugs, and situations involving health risks or structural damage.

ROACH PREVENTION CHECKLIST
German cockroaches and American cockroaches enter homes seeking food, water, and warmth. Prevention starts with eliminating attractants.
- Store all dry food in sealed glass or hard plastic containers; roaches can chew through thin cardboard.
- Wipe kitchen counters and stovetops every night; even grease film feeds roaches.
- Fix leaky faucets and pipes; roaches can survive weeks without food but only days without water.
- Take kitchen trash out daily; use a bin with a tight lid.
- Seal cracks around baseboards, pipe penetrations, and cabinet backs with silicone caulk.
- Vacuum crumbs behind the refrigerator and stove monthly.
- Boric acid powder applied thinly along cracks and behind appliances kills roaches that walk through it; keep away from food prep surfaces and pets.
- Diatomaceous earth (food-grade) in wall voids damages roach exoskeletons; wear a dust mask when applying.
- Sticky traps near suspected entry points help monitor whether numbers are dropping.
- If you see roaches during the day, the population may already be large—consider professional inspection.

ANT TRICKS USING CHEAP HOUSEHOLD ITEMS
Ants follow scent trails laid by scout ants. Killing visible ants without removing the trail or nest often fails.
- Wipe ant trails with soapy water (dish soap breaks the pheromone trail).
- A 50/50 mix of white vinegar and water sprayed along baseboards disrupts trails.
- Borax bait: mix 1 part borax with 3 parts sugar and enough water to make a paste; place pea-sized amounts on cardboard near ant activity (not where pets or children can reach).
- Caulk gaps around windows, doors, and utility lines.
- Carpenter ants indicate moisture-damaged wood; fixing leaks matters more than sprays.

MOSQUITO AND GNAT SUMMER GUIDE
Mosquitoes breed in standing water; gnats often come from overwatered houseplants.
- Empty birdbaths, clogged gutters, and flower-pot saucers at least weekly.
- Repair torn window and door screens before warm weather.
- Fans on porches disrupt weak-flying mosquitoes.
- For gnats indoors: let houseplant soil dry between waterings; sticky yellow traps catch adults.

MOUSE PREVENTION AROUND THE HOME
House mice fit through gaps as small as a dime.
- Steel wool plus caulk blocks small holes around pipes and cables.
- Store pantry goods in metal or thick plastic containers.
- Snap traps baited with peanut butter placed perpendicular to walls are effective.
- Avoid poison baits indoors if pets or grandchildren visit.

WASP PREVENTION TIPS
- In spring, inspect eaves and porch ceilings for small starter nests; removing a golf-ball-sized nest early is safer.
- Seal trash cans tightly; yellow jackets scavenge protein and sweets.
- If allergic to stings, carry prescribed epinephrine and hire professionals for nest removal on structures.

FLEA AND BED BUG CAUTION GUIDE
Fleas: vacuum daily, wash pet bedding in hot water, treat pets with veterinarian-approved products.
Bed bugs: hide in mattress seams and bed frames; DIY foggers often fail. Encasements on mattress and box spring help. Bed bug infestations commonly require licensed pest professionals.

HYDROGEN PEROXIDE PEST USES
Standard 3% hydrogen peroxide helps clean surfaces after pest cleanup. Not a reliable insecticide for cockroaches or bed bugs at household concentrations. Do not mix with vinegar in the same bottle.

BAKING SODA, VINEGAR, BORAX, SALT, AND DAWN COMBINATIONS
Vinegar cleans and removes ant trails. Borax is effective in proper bait formulations. Dawn in water makes insecticidal soap for soft-bodied aphids when sprayed directly. Never mix bleach with ammonia or vinegar.

OUTDOOR PEST PREVENTION
Trim tree branches away from roof lines. Install door sweeps. Compost bins should be closed. Mulch should not touch foundation siding—leave a 6-inch gap.

MONTHLY PEST-PROOFING CHECKLIST
Week 1: Kitchen audit—seal food, wipe grease, check under sink for leaks.
Week 2: Perimeter walk—screens, door sweeps, foundation gaps.
Week 3: Bathroom and laundry—fix drips, clean drains.
Week 4: Attic/garage/basement scan—droppings, wasp starter nests.

WHEN TO CALL A PROFESSIONAL
Call licensed pest control when bed bugs are confirmed, roaches are seen daily in multiple rooms, stinging insect nests are inside walls, termite mud tubes appear, or anyone has anaphylaxis risk from stings.

EXTERMINATOR BILL AVOIDANCE — SAVINGS TRACKER
General pest visit: $150–$400 per treatment. Bed bug treatment: $1,000–$3,000+. Monthly DIY supply budget often runs $15–$40. Track savings by logging date, pest issue, DIY action, cost of supplies, and estimated professional quote.

SAFETY NOTES FOR SENIORS
Read all product labels. Wear gloves when handling traps or cleaning droppings. Ventilate rooms when using sprays. Keep grandchildren and pets away from baits.
